How it works

Three steps, no jargon

🧭 1. Choose a route

Like a transit map: tell it where you're starting from (finished series, active channel, or pilot), and it points you to the platform.

🔎 2. Check the source

Like reading a contract before signing: each entry links to the official site, because country, account requirements, and rights can vary.

✉️ 3. Prepare the pitch

A pitch is the short proposal you send to whoever decides. Like a business card: title, one-line story, episodes, sample, and proof that you own the rights.

🤝 What the kit doesn't promise

It doesn't promise anyone will accept your series or how much you'll earn: "up to US$ X" is the cap, not the typical amount. It won't send an email or sign you up for anything.

YouTube Partner ProgramCeiling: ad shareOpen in many countries

Real cases

Two cases, the same kind of video, opposite results

What decides it is not the idea: it is who pays to bring the viewers in.

Taiwan · US$ 1,265 for one episode

A creator in Taiwan posts an AI drama series on Instagram. In November one episode passed 10,000 likes and earned over US$ 1,200 — but the money came from the AI tool he used (the program that pays by likes), not from Instagram or a drama app.

  • He builds each episode with 4 tools: video (Seedance inside OiiOii), ChatGPT, Suno (music) and CapCut (editing).
  • Cost: about US$ 15 an episode.
  • The tool’s payout table: 500 likes = US$ 65; 10,000 likes = US$ 1,265.
  • He warns it took 6 months to build loyal fans, every episode needs a new hook in the first 3 seconds, and the platform pays too much to last.

China · US$ 3 back from 12 episodes

A creator made a 12-episode AI drama on her own for about US$ 250. After almost 50,000 views she got back about US$ 3. In the first half of the year only about 1 in 100 new AI dramas earned its money back.

  • There, a drama is only seen if someone pays for ads to bring viewers — and small teams rarely can.
  • On a drama app, someone pays to bring the audience. In a tool program, the tool pays you to show the tool.

AI character · the ad that never got paid

An AI heroine of a Chinese drama, with over 400,000 fans, listed an ad price of up to US$ 39,000. Her first real deal was worth about 10,000. In an ad for colored contact lenses she said she wore them all day — but an AI can’t wear lenses.

  • The video was taken down, her team was never paid, and on 13 November the regulator fined the team the same amount as the ad fee.
  • If you try it: put the product inside the story, never let an AI character say it used the product, and turn on the paid-promotion label.

Common questions

Before you get started

Do I need to know how to code?

No. Paste the prompt and answer the questions.

Will this work for me if I live in Brazil?

Partly. Some programs are only open in certain countries, and the AI checks each one's source. The list tells you when the page doesn't say which countries.

Does it guarantee I'll earn money?

No. The kit shows where you can try and what each place requires.

Can I send the full script?

Better not. The checklist suggests a short pitch with a one-line story and two or three finished episodes shared via a private link.

How much does it cost?

Nothing. The kit and this guide are free. You only need the AI you already use.

What if they ask me to pay?

Don't pay. The checklist reminds you that no one should charge you to "get you into" an app.
